## What it is

A North Star Metric (NSM) is the single number a team rallies around because it best reflects the value customers actually get from the product — and, chosen well, it predicts long-term business success. Examples: Spotify's time spent listening, Airbnb's nights booked, Slack's messages sent between teammates. Each one captures customer value directly; revenue and growth tend to follow when the North Star moves in the right direction.

A good North Star sits above feature-level metrics but below pure business outcomes like revenue — it's the bridge between "customers are getting value" and "the business is growing."

## When to use it

Introduce a North Star Metric when multiple teams are shipping independently and need one shared definition of success to align around — it prevents each team from optimizing a local metric (signups, session count, feature adoption) that doesn't actually move the business. It's also useful during planning and roadmap reviews as a forcing question: "does this initiative move the North Star, or just a vanity number next to it?"

## Common pitfalls

- **Picking a metric that's really a business output.** Revenue, MRR, and similar figures are lagging outcomes, not signals of customer value — they make a poor North Star because a team can't directly act on them.
- **Choosing something too easy to game.** A metric like "signups" can be inflated with marketing spend without any real increase in value delivered, which defeats the purpose.
- **Treating the North Star as the only metric that matters.** It needs supporting input metrics (the specific levers teams can pull) or it becomes too abstract to act on day-to-day.
- **Never revisiting it.** A North Star chosen at seed stage may stop reflecting value as the product and business model mature — it's worth re-examining, not treated as permanent.
