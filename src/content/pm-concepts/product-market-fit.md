## What it is

Product-market fit (PMF) is the point at which a product satisfies real market demand strongly enough that growth starts to feel pulled by customers rather than pushed by the team — word-of-mouth increases, retention stabilizes, and acquisition stops requiring constant paid effort to sustain. Marc Andreessen described it as the moment "the market pulls product out of the startup," as opposed to the earlier stage where every sale requires convincing.

PMF isn't a single binary event so much as a threshold a product crosses — and it can be lost again if the market shifts or the product stops evolving with it.

## When to use it

PMF is the central question before pre-PMF, and the framing shifts almost everything about how a team should operate at that stage — for example, Sean Ellis's "40% test" asks users how they'd feel if they could no longer use the product; if 40%+ say "very disappointed," that's historically correlated with having reached fit. Retention curves that flatten out (instead of decaying to zero) are another common signal worth tracking over cohorts.

Before PMF, the priority is fast iteration and willingness to pivot on positioning, audience, or even the core product. After PMF, the priority typically shifts toward scaling what's working — growth, operations, and defensibility.

## Common pitfalls

- **Optimizing for growth before reaching fit.** Scaling acquisition on a product people don't retain just accelerates churn and burns budget faster.
- **Declaring PMF from a small, unrepresentative sample.** A handful of enthusiastic early adopters isn't the same as demand from the actual target market.
- **Treating PMF as permanent.** Competitive entry, market shifts, or platform changes can erode fit that once existed — retention and word-of-mouth need to be watched continuously, not confirmed once and forgotten.
