## What it is

An Opportunity Solution Tree, developed by Teresa Torres, is a visual map that connects a single desired outcome to the customer opportunities (needs, pain points, or desires) that could drive it, the possible solutions for each opportunity, and the experiments used to test those solutions before committing to build. The structure reads top to bottom:

1. **Outcome** — the measurable result you're trying to move (e.g., "increase week-4 retention").
2. **Opportunities** — problems or needs, usually surfaced through continuous customer interviews, that stand between users and that outcome.
3. **Solutions** — multiple possible ways to address a given opportunity, not just the first idea that comes up.
4. **Experiments** — small, fast tests that generate evidence about whether a solution is worth building.

The tree format makes two things visible that a flat backlog hides: how many different opportunities you've actually considered for an outcome, and how many different solutions you've considered for each opportunity — rather than jumping from outcome straight to a single solution.

## When to use it

Use it as an ongoing discovery habit — most effective when paired with weekly customer touchpoints — to keep a product team grounded in real opportunities instead of drifting toward whatever solution is easiest to build or most requested by the loudest stakeholder. It's particularly useful for teams that already have a clear outcome metric but are debating what to build next to move it.

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping straight from outcome to solution.** Without mapping opportunities first, teams end up debating features instead of debating which customer problem is actually worth solving.
- **Only ever considering one solution per opportunity.** The tree's value comes from branching — generating multiple solution options and comparing them, not just formalizing the first idea.
- **Letting the tree go stale.** It's meant to be a living artifact updated as new interview data comes in, not a one-time planning exercise archived after a single workshop.
