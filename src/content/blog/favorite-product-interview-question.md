**"What's your favorite product, and why?"** is one of the most common questions in a product management interview.

At first, it sounds simple. You just need to name a product you like and explain why. But interviewers are usually evaluating much more than your product preference. Your answer can reveal how you think about products, users, product decisions, and your own experiences as a product manager.

A strong answer should demonstrate three things:

1. **Passion** — You genuinely care about the product.
2. **Perspective** — You have a thoughtful and arguable point of view.
3. **Personality** — Your answer gives the interviewer a sense of who you are.

Let's break down how to approach the question and build a compelling answer.

## Why Do Interviewers Ask About Your Favorite Product?

The favorite product interview question is not simply a test of whether you know a popular app.

Interviewers use it to understand your **product sense**, communication skills, and ability to form opinions about products. They want to see whether you can move beyond statements such as "This product is easy to use" and explain *why* specific product decisions matter.

Your answer should show that you can:

- Identify what makes a product valuable.
- Understand the user problem it solves.
- Explain why certain product decisions work.
- Form an independent product opinion.
- Connect product observations to your own experiences.

The goal is not to select the "best" product. The goal is to choose a product that allows you to demonstrate how you think.

## 1. Show Genuine Passion

The first rule is simple: **choose a product you genuinely care about.**

Don't select a product merely because you think it will impress the interviewer.

If you rarely use a product, your lack of familiarity will often become obvious when the interviewer asks follow-up questions. On the other hand, when you genuinely love a product, you'll naturally have examples, opinions, and experiences to discuss.

Think about products you use frequently, such as:

- Slack
- Spotify
- Google Maps
- Notion
- WhatsApp
- Airbnb
- Amazon
- ChatGPT
- Duolingo
- Figma

The specific product matters less than your ability to explain *why* you care about it.

### A Useful Test

Ask yourself:

> "If the interviewer asked me five more questions about this product, would I still have interesting things to say?"

If the answer is yes, you probably have a good candidate.

## 2. Develop an Arguable Perspective

This is where many candidates make their biggest mistake.

A weak answer might be:

> "Google Maps is my favorite product because it helps me navigate efficiently."

That's true, but it's also generic. Almost anyone could say it.

Instead, develop an **arguable product thesis**—an opinion that is specific enough for another person to disagree with.

For example:

> "Google Maps isn't my favorite because it gives me directions. It's my favorite because it has transformed navigation from a destination-finding problem into a decision-making product."

That statement gives you something deeper to discuss.

You can then explain how Google Maps helps users make decisions about routes, traffic, restaurants, businesses, public transportation, and timing—not simply find a location.

### What Makes a Good Product Perspective?

A strong perspective is:

- **Specific:** It focuses on one meaningful product insight.
- **Arguable:** Another product manager could reasonably disagree.
- **Relevant:** It connects to user value or product strategy.
- **Supported:** You can explain it with concrete examples.

Try completing this sentence:

> **"I think [product] succeeds because ________, and this is more important than ________."**

This forces you to develop a point of view rather than simply list features.

## 3. Let Your Personality Come Through

The question asks about **your** favorite product.

That means the interviewer also wants to learn something about you.

Don't turn your answer into a product teardown that sounds like a case study. Explain why the product resonates with you personally.

For example:

> "I love products that make complex workflows feel simple. That's something I try to bring into the products I work on, so I naturally pay attention to how teams reduce friction for users."

Now the answer tells the interviewer something about your product philosophy.

You can also discuss:

- How you discovered the product.
- How frequently you use it.
- A memorable experience you had with it.
- A feature you particularly appreciate.
- What you would learn from the product as a PM.
- How the product philosophy influences your own work.

Your personality makes the answer memorable.

## A Simple Framework for Your Answer

A useful structure is:

### 1. Name the Product

Start directly.

> "My favorite product is Slack."

### 2. Explain Your Personal Connection

Explain why you genuinely use or appreciate it.

> "I use Slack every day, and I particularly enjoy how it makes workplace communication feel more human."

### 3. State Your Product Thesis

Give your arguable perspective.

> "My view is that Slack's success isn't just about communication efficiency. It's about bringing delight into workplace collaboration."

### 4. Support It With Examples

Discuss specific product choices that support your thesis.

For Slack, you could discuss:

- Emojis and reactions.
- GIFs and informal communication.
- Channels and communities.
- Integrations.
- Notifications and workflow customization.

Don't simply list features. Explain **why they matter to users**.

### 5. Connect It to Your PM Philosophy

Finish by explaining what the product teaches you.

> "As a product manager, I value products that solve functional problems while also creating positive emotional experiences. Slack reminds me that workplace software doesn't have to feel purely transactional."

This creates a natural connection between your favorite product and your approach to product management.

## Example: A Strong Favorite Product Answer

Here's an example using Slack:

> **"My favorite product is Slack. I use it every day, but what I particularly appreciate is how Slack has brought delight into workplace communication.**
>
> **My perspective is that delight is an underrated reason for Slack's success. Workplace software traditionally focuses on efficiency and functionality, but Slack makes collaboration feel more human through things like emojis, reactions, GIFs, and informal conversations. These details may seem small, but collectively they change how people experience work.**
>
> **That's something I personally value as a product manager. I believe great products shouldn't only solve a functional problem; they should also create an experience people enjoy using. I try to bring that same mindset into the products and teams I work with.**
>
> **That's why Slack stands out to me—not simply because it helps teams communicate, but because it makes collaboration more enjoyable."**

Notice what makes the answer effective:

- **Passion:** The candidate clearly uses and enjoys Slack.
- **Perspective:** The thesis is that user delight contributes to Slack's success.
- **Personality:** The answer reveals the candidate's own product philosophy.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Choosing a Product You Don't Know Well

Don't choose a famous product just because you think the interviewer will like it.

A product you use every day gives you much more material for follow-up questions.

### Giving a Generic Reason

Statements such as "It's easy to use," "It saves time," or "It has a great UI" are not enough on their own.

Explain **what makes the product effective and why that matters**.

### Listing Features

A feature list is not a product analysis.

Instead of saying:

> "I like Spotify because it has playlists, recommendations, podcasts, and offline downloads."

Explain the underlying product value:

> "Spotify is particularly strong at helping users reduce the effort required to discover what they want to listen to."

### Trying to Guess the "Correct" Product

There is no universally correct favorite product.

Interviewers aren't looking for the product they personally use. They're looking for a well-reasoned point of view.

### Overcomplicating the Answer

You don't need a 15-minute product teardown.

Aim for a concise answer—typically around **1–2 minutes** initially—and let the interviewer decide where to go deeper.

## How to Prepare Before Your Interview

Prepare two or three favorite products before the interview.

For each product, write down:

- Why you genuinely like it.
- The primary user problem it solves.
- Your strongest product insight.
- One or two features that support your opinion.
- What you would improve.
- What the product teaches you as a PM.

Then practice explaining each product without memorizing a script.

The goal is to sound **prepared, not rehearsed**.

## Final Takeaway

The best answer to **"What's your favorite product?"** isn't necessarily the most sophisticated product analysis.

It's the answer that combines **genuine passion, a distinctive perspective, and personality**.

Choose a product you actually care about. Develop an opinion that goes beyond obvious observations. Support that opinion with specific product examples. Finally, connect the product to the way you think about product management.

If you can do those three things—**passion, perspective, and personality**—you'll turn a simple interview question into an opportunity to demonstrate strong product sense.

## Quick Checklist

Before your product management interview, make sure your answer has:

- [ ] A product you genuinely use or care about.
- [ ] A clear user problem the product solves.
- [ ] A specific and arguable product thesis.
- [ ] Concrete examples supporting your thesis.
- [ ] Your personal connection to the product.
- [ ] A connection to your product management philosophy.
- [ ] A concise, conversational delivery.
